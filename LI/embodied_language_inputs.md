# embodied_language_inputs.md
## Embodied and Tactile Forms of Language-Use (Exploratory)

---

## Status of this document

This document captures an **exploratory extension of Language-Use Inputs (LUIs)**
to include embodied, tactile, and spatial forms of inscription.

It is intentionally **unprefixed**.

The absence of a numeric prefix signals that:
- this material is derivative of existing LI assumptions
- its final layer placement is not yet canonized
- semantic coherence is being tested before structural commitment

This document may later be merged, promoted, or refactored
once its relationship to other LI layers stabilizes.

---

## Purpose

The purpose of this document is **not** to specify devices,
input hardware, or interface features.

Instead, it records a **structural allowance** within the Language Infrastructure:

> Human language-use is not limited to symbolic text entry.
> It may be embodied, tactile, spatial, and gestural,
> provided it produces time-sequenced, attributable continuity traces.

This allowance follows from existing LI commitments
and does not introduce new primitives.

---

## Background observation

Humans have historically used **physical inscription** as a primary means of thinking.

Examples include:
- handwriting
- diagramming
- sketching with symbolic intent
- spatial arrangement of marks
- revision through erasure and overwriting

These practices are not merely representational.
They are **cognitive acts** that shape attention, memory, and abstraction.

The act of inscription matters, not only the resulting symbols.

---

## Language-use beyond symbolic typing

Within IAM, a Language-Use Input (LUI) is defined by its role, not its modality.

An LUI:
- originates from the human
- unfolds over time
- is attributable
- produces an irreversible registration

Typing satisfies these conditions, but it is not exclusive.

**Embodied inscription** may also satisfy them.

---

## Embodied inscription as continuity trace

Physical acts such as handwriting or drawing:

- unfold temporally
- involve motor intention
- encode hesitation, emphasis, revision, and rhythm
- often preserve spatial relationships meaningful to the thinker

When captured as time-sequenced input,
these acts produce continuity traces analogous to typed text.

The continuity substrate must therefore permit,
and not structurally forbid,
such forms of language-use.

---

## Constraint: Modality neutrality of LUIs

**Constraint**

The Language Infrastructure must remain neutral
with respect to the physical modality of language-use input.

It must not:
- privilege keyboard-based text as the only legitimate LUI
- collapse embodied input into post-hoc symbolic summaries
- treat physical inscription as secondary or decorative

Any modality that satisfies the core properties of an LUI
must be representable within the continuity substrate.

---

## Relationship to typing-first interfaces

Typing remains a first-class LUI because it is:
- low-ceremony
- widely available
- naturally time-sequenced
- cognitively familiar

This document does **not** weaken that claim.

Instead, it generalizes the underlying principle:

> **Time-sequenced human inscription is the continuity primitive.**

Typing is one instantiation.
Handwriting and sketch-language hybrids are others.

---

## Implications for interfaces (non-prescriptive)

This document does not mandate support for any specific embodiment.

It only asserts that:
- interfaces must not preclude embodied language-use
- future embodiments may surface these modalities
- reduction of embodied input to static text is a lossy transformation

Interpretation, transcription, or abstraction may occur later,
but original traces must remain inspectable.

---

## Non-goals and exclusions

This document does **not**:
- specify recognition algorithms
- require handwriting-to-text conversion
- define spatial semantics
- prescribe UI affordances
- assert empirical cognitive superiority claims

It records a **structural opening**, not an implementation plan.

---

## Provisional invariant

If IAM preserves cognitive continuity rather than symbolic snapshots,
then it must allow language-use inputs that are:

- embodied
- time-sequenced
- attributable
- irreducible to static symbols without loss

Any infrastructure that forbids these a priori
narrows cognition prematurely.
