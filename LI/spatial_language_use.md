# spatial_language_use.md
## Spatial Forms of Language-Use (Exploratory)

---

## Status of this document

This document records an **early exploratory inquiry** into whether
**spatial arrangement and movement can function as language-use inputs**
within the IAM Language Infrastructure.

It is intentionally **unprefixed** and **lightweight**.

The goal is not to assert a new layer or constraint,
but to test whether existing LI commitments already
permit spatially grounded forms of meaning-making.

---

## Purpose

The purpose of this document is to ask a narrow question:

> Can language-use be partially encoded in spatial relationships,
> rather than exclusively in symbolic sequences?

This document does not prescribe interfaces, devices, or embodiments.
It does not introduce new primitives.

It exists to test semantic compatibility.

---

## Background observation

Humans frequently think using space.

Examples include:
- arranging ideas on a page
- grouping, circling, or isolating concepts
- positioning marks relative to one another
- revisiting physical locations associated with prior thought
- using proximity, orientation, or containment to convey meaning

In these cases, meaning is not carried solely by symbols,
but by **where things are placed and how they relate in space**.

---

## Spatial arrangement as language-use

Within IAM, a Language-Use Input (LUI) is defined by:
- human origin
- time-sequenced action
- attribution
- registration into continuity

Spatial acts such as:
- placing
- moving
- grouping
- orienting

may satisfy these properties when they are:
- intentional
- temporally extended
- meaning-bearing for the human performing them

This suggests that **space itself may participate in language-use**,
not merely as a container, but as a representational medium.

---

## Relationship to embodied language inputs

This exploration extends, but does not supersede,
the allowance for embodied and tactile language-use.

Spatial language-use differs in that:
- meaning may be distributed across locations
- inspection may involve re-entering a configuration
- continuity may be indexed by both time and position

No conflict with existing LI assumptions is introduced.

---

## Continuity and spatial re-entry

If continuity preserves the path of thinking,
then re-entering a prior spatial configuration
may function analogously to re-reading prior text.

In such cases:
- memory is accessed by spatial traversal
- inspection occurs through orientation and movement
- meaning is recalled through spatial cues

This document does not assert this as a requirement,
only as a compatible possibility.

---

## Non-goals

This document does **not**:
- define spatial semantics
- prescribe dimensionality (2D vs 3D)
- mandate spatial interfaces
- claim empirical cognitive advantages
- specify capture or storage mechanisms

It remains strictly exploratory.

---

## Provisional question (kept open)

If IAM preserves continuity of thinking,
and if some thinking unfolds spatially,
then to what extent must continuity
remain **spatially legible** as well as temporally ordered?

This question is intentionally left unanswered.

---

This document may:

- be retired if no practical consequences emerge
- or expand if spatial thinking proves structurally important

No promotion criteria are asserted at this stage.
