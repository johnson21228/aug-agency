Continuity Strata

Why strata are necessary

Most computational systems collapse continuity, identity, and meaning into a single representational layer. Time becomes metadata, memory becomes storage, and meaning becomes an inferred property of data.

IAM rejects this collapse.

Preserving human continuity of thought over time requires introducing constraints in a specific order, where each constraint depends on the previous one holding. These are not implementation stages, maturity levels, or feature tiers. They are conceptual strata.

Each stratum answers a single question:

What must already be true for higher-order continuity to remain intelligible?

If a lower stratum fails, higher strata do not merely degrade; they become incoherent. What follows is therefore a dependency ladder, not a pipeline.

⸻

Stratum 0: Numeric Continuity

Question answered:
What exists before language, meaning, or interpretation?

At the base of the architecture is numeric continuity: ordering, adjacency, and identity expressed without semantics. This stratum establishes that there is a before and after, a next and a previous, without asserting what any element means.

This layer is deliberately semantic-free. It does not know about language, topics, or interpretation. Its purpose is to guarantee that continuity can exist independently of meaning.

If continuity is encoded semantically at this level, rebuildability becomes impossible. Any change in interpretation would rewrite time itself.

⸻

Stratum 1: Capture and Provenance

Question answered:
What are the irreducible facts of language use?

At this stratum, language-use events (LUIs) are captured as append-only facts. Each event records that something was said, written, or produced, by a source, at a moment in time.

This is the first point at which digital language use acquires a temporal character. Language is not treated as timeless content, but as something that occurred.

This stratum is where Being-in-Time becomes digitally preservable: not as philosophy, but as ordered, revisit-able history.

If capture is lossy, mutable, or overwritten, all higher continuity collapses into reconstruction and inference.

⸻

Stratum 2: Stable Identity and Referential Integrity

Question answered:
What can be pointed to over time?

Continuity requires the ability to return—not just to similar content, but to the same event. This stratum establishes stable identity grounded solely in capture provenance.

Identity here is not semantic. It does not depend on embeddings, clustering, or interpretation. It depends only on the fact that an event occurred and was captured.

Without this stratum, reflexivity fails: annotations, return points, and references cannot survive rebuilds, and continuity degrades into approximation.

⸻

Stratum 3: Continuity Geometry

Question answered:
How can time be navigated without interpretation?

This stratum introduces structure over events without introducing meaning. Adjacency, neighborhoods, return paths, and regions are constructed to make continuity usable without flattening it into summaries or topics.

This is where “return” becomes operational. One can move through prior reasoning without the system asserting what that reasoning means.

If semantic interpretation enters at this stratum, continuity becomes hostage to model choice. Navigation turns into inference, and rebuildability is lost.

⸻

Stratum 4: Provenance Overlays

Question answered:
How does language reattach to continuity?

Here, captured language spans and optional semantic coordinates are associated with events and regions in continuity space. Meaning appears, but only as overlay.

These overlays are explicitly versioned, replaceable, and non-authoritative. They exist to support human re-entry, not to define truth.

Most systems treat this stratum as foundational. IAM treats it as optional.

If overlays are allowed to define identity or structure, semantic drift corrupts continuity.

⸻

Stratum 5: Slices and Task-Oriented Views

Question answered:
How can continuity be made usable without collapsing it?

Slices are derived selections of events, spans, and coordinates assembled for specific tasks: prompting, review, reflection, or work-in-progress.

They are conveniences, not memory. They trade completeness for usability while retaining stable references back to capture and continuity geometry.

If slices become authoritative, the system devolves into a workspace tool rather than a continuity-preserving system.

⸻

Stratum 6: Human-Oriented Orchestration

Question answered:
How does a human live inside this system without losing themselves?

At the highest stratum, IAM coordinates continuity geometry, provenance overlays, and slices to support re-entry, reflection, and judgment.

This stratum introduces no new truth. It arranges what already exists so that humans can resume thought, not merely retrieve information.

Continuity gains meaning here only through human judgment expressed over time, captured again as language-use events.

⸻

What the strata collectively guarantee

Taken together, the strata ensure that:
	•	Time is preserved before meaning
	•	Identity survives interpretation
	•	Continuity remains navigable without inference
	•	Meaning can evolve without rewriting history
	•	Human judgment remains the sole source of curvature

This ordering is not optional. Any architecture that violates it may appear to work, but will fail under rebuild, revision, or sustained use.
