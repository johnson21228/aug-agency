# Agent vs Human

This document defines the structural distinction between **agents** and **humans** in the context of continuity, agency, and thinking support systems.

## Agents

An **agent** is a system that:
- Executes actions on behalf of a user
- Operates continuously or autonomously
- Optimizes toward goals, rewards, or predicted outcomes
- Acts without requiring return or re-entry

Agents are defined by **execution capacity**.  
They are effective at acting, scheduling, retrieving, optimizing, and performing tasks.

Agents do not require continuity of thought.  
They require coherence within bounded decision windows.

## Humans

A **human** is a being whose agency depends on:
- The ability to form intentions
- The ability to revise intentions
- The ability to carry meaning forward over time

Humans do not operate solely through execution.  
Human agency depends on **continuity**.

Human thinking unfolds across time through:
- Imagination (exploration of possibilities)
- Attention (ability to return)
- Meaning (integration across experience)

## Structural Difference

| Dimension | Agent | Human |
|--------|-------|-------|
| Primary function | Execution | Agency |
| Time horizon | Immediate / bounded | Longitudinal |
| Dependency | Optimization | Continuity |
| Failure mode | Inefficiency | Loss of agency |
| Support needed | Compute | Continuity infrastructure |

## Implication

As environments become more agentic, the human role shifts away from execution toward:
- Judgment
- Sense-making
- Direction-setting

These capacities **cannot function without continuity**.

Therefore, tools that serve humans must support continuity rather than compete with agency.

IAM is positioned to support humans, not agents.
