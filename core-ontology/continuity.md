# Continuity

**Continuity** is the structural condition that allows activity, thought, or intention to persist and be re-entered across time.

Continuity is not memory.  
Continuity is not coherence.  
Continuity is not storage.

Continuity governs **return**, not recall.

## Definition

Continuity is the preservation of structural relationships across time such that prior activity may be re-entered without reconstruction or reinterpretation.

## What Continuity Is Not

- Not semantic meaning
- Not summarized history
- Not inferred intent
- Not optimized state
- Not narrative coherence

Continuity exists **prior to interpretation**.

## Properties of Continuity

Continuity preserves:
- Temporal ordering
- Adjacency
- Recurrence
- Boundaries
- Stability of regions
- Re-entry points

Continuity does not preserve meaning.  
Meaning re-emerges only when a human returns.

## Continuity vs Coherence

- **Coherence**: local consistency within an interaction
- **Continuity**: longitudinal persistence across interactions

Systems may be coherent without being continuous.  
Humans cannot maintain agency without continuity.

## Structural Realization

Continuity is realized as a non-semantic structure within the **Continuity Atlas**, the persisted substrate that holds ordering, regions, adjacency, and return points.

Continuity is accessed and navigated through **CAP (Continuity Access Protocol)**, which defines permitted operations over Atlas identifiers without semantic interpretation.

## Role in IAM

IAM treats continuity as a first-class concern.  
It preserves continuity without performing interpretation.

Continuity is the substrate upon which imagination, attention, and meaning unfold.
