# Background

Digital systems increasingly act on behalf of users through automation, agents, and generative models. These systems are commonly designed to interpret language, infer intent, and optimize actions or outputs. While effective for task execution, such approaches risk displacing human agency by substituting inferred objectives for explicit intention.

Human thinking, however, unfolds over time. It depends on the ability to return to prior activity, revise partial understandings, and integrate experience longitudinally. Conventional digital systems treat interaction as episodic, prioritizing immediate coherence and response quality over continuity.

Existing memory systems store data or retrieve information but do not preserve the structural relationships required for re-entry into prior thinking. Retrieval-augmented generation systems externalize knowledge semantically, while agent frameworks emphasize autonomous execution. Neither approach is designed to preserve the conditions under which human thought persists.

Furthermore, systems that infer meaning or optimize engagement intrude into the interior space required for thinking, raising privacy concerns that cannot be addressed solely through policy or access controls.

Accordingly, there exists a need for systems that support human agency by preserving continuity without semantic interpretation, inference, or optimization, thereby maintaining privacy and enabling sustained human thought in increasingly agentic digital environments.
