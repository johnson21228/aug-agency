BACKGROUND

Modern computational systems increasingly mediate human activity across time through conversational interfaces, assistive tools, and automated processes. While such systems may support task execution, retrieval, or optimization, they generally treat interaction as episodic rather than continuous. Each session is resolved independently, requiring reconstruction of context, intent, or meaning in order to proceed.

Existing approaches to continuity rely on the persistence of semantic representations, including stored text, inferred intent, embeddings, summaries, or user profiles. Under these approaches, continuity is achieved only by retaining information that is interpretable in principle. This conflation of continuity with semantics is widely assumed in prior systems and architectures.

However, semantic persistence introduces structural risks. When continuity is implemented through interpretable representations, retained state becomes inspectable, optimizable, and economically extractable. Such systems create an intrinsic control surface for profiling, personalization, persuasion, or behavioral optimization that arises from the structure of the representation itself, rather than from any particular operator intent.

Attempts to mitigate these risks through governance mechanisms—such as access controls, encryption, anonymization, or consent frameworks—do not eliminate the underlying structural property that semantic meaning is retained and resolvable in principle. As a result, governance-based safeguards are insufficient to render semantic continuity structurally safe.

At the same time, continuity across time is increasingly required for human agency. As interaction shifts toward conversational and assistive modalities, the ability to resume thought, maintain directionality, and carry intention forward becomes a prerequisite for meaningful human engagement. Without continuity, interaction collapses into isolated prompts and responses, eroding authorship and reflective judgment even in the presence of highly capable automation.

Furthermore, systems that preserve continuity through semantic inference or optimization are structurally incapable of maintaining continuity without intruding into the interior space required for human thinking, creating privacy risks that cannot be resolved solely through policy or access controls.

Accordingly, there exists a need for a system that provides longitudinal continuity without semantic persistence. Such a system must preserve continuity across time while being structurally incapable of storing, resolving, or deriving semantic meaning, identity, authorship, or interpretive content. The present disclosure addresses this need.
