# Martin Heidegger — Being-in-Time as the Ground of Judgment

## Overview

Martin Heidegger’s *Being and Time* radicalizes the role of time in cognition by rejecting the idea that time is a feature added to an otherwise complete account of mind. Temporality is not an attribute of judgment; it is the condition that makes judgment possible at all. Understanding, discernment, and decision arise only because human existence (*Dasein*) is fundamentally temporal.

Judgment, in this framework, is not an evaluative act performed by a subject upon representations. It is an expression of *being-in-time*: the way an entity whose existence is stretched between past and future discloses meaning and acts within the world.

---

## Being-in-the-World and the Rejection of Detached Judgment

Heidegger begins by rejecting the model of cognition as a relation between an inner subject and outer objects. Human existence is not first a spectator and only later an actor. It is always already *being-in-the-world*.

This has immediate consequences for judgment. Judgment is not the application of criteria to neutral data. It is a mode of practical involvement within a meaningful situation. One judges from within a world that already matters, not from a position outside it.

Detached evaluation is a derivative mode of understanding. Primary understanding is engaged, situated, and temporal.

---

## Temporality as the Structure of Understanding

Heidegger argues that *Dasein* exists as a unified temporal structure composed of three inseparable moments:

- **Thrownness (Geworfenheit):** the fact that one always finds oneself already in a situation shaped by a past one did not choose  
- **Projection (Entwurf):** the orientation toward future possibilities that gives direction to action  
- **Falling (Verfallen):** absorption in present concerns and practices  

These are not stages or components. They are aspects of a single temporal phenomenon. Human existence is stretched between what has been and what is possible, and it is only within this stretch that understanding occurs.

Judgment is grounded in this structure. To judge is to take a stand within a field of possibilities shaped by past commitments and future orientation.

---

## Understanding as Ahead-of-Itself

For Heidegger, understanding is fundamentally anticipatory. One does not first perceive a situation and then decide what it means. One always already understands situations in terms of possible actions and outcomes.

This “ahead-of-itself” structure means that judgment is not retrospective. It is prospective. It does not primarily assess what is the case; it orients action toward what may be.

Judgment, therefore, cannot be reduced to evaluation of present facts. It depends on how the future is opened or foreclosed by the way the situation is understood.

---

## Care as the Ground of Judgment

Heidegger identifies *care* (*Sorge*) as the basic structure of *Dasein*. Care is not an emotion or preference. It names the fact that existence matters to itself over time.

Because one’s being is at stake in what one does, judgment is never neutral. It is always bound up with concern, responsibility, and consequence. One judges not as an observer, but as someone whose future is implicated in the judgment.

This does not make judgment subjective. It makes it situated. Judgment arises from involvement, not from detachment.

---

## Interpretation and the Hermeneutic Circle

Understanding, for Heidegger, always operates within a hermeneutic circle: one interprets the present in light of a prior understanding, which is itself shaped by past experience and future orientation.

Judgment occurs within this circle. It does not escape it. To judge is to refine and redirect an existing understanding, not to begin from zero.

This makes judgment historically and personally conditioned without making it arbitrary. Continuity is not a flaw; it is the medium of intelligibility.

---

## The Irreversibility of Existential Time

Existential time is irreversible. One cannot return to a prior understanding except as transformed by what has occurred since. Each judgment alters the horizon within which future judgments will occur.

This irreversibility undermines any attempt to reconstruct judgment after the fact. Once a judgment has been made, the conditions that gave rise to it have already been altered.

Judgment cannot be replayed. It can only be carried forward.

---

## Against Representation-Centered Models of Judgment

Heidegger’s analysis constitutes a decisive rejection of models that treat judgment as the manipulation of representations. Such models assume that meaning is present in objects and that judgment consists in selecting or evaluating that meaning.

For Heidegger, meaning arises from use, involvement, and temporal orientation. Representation is a secondary abstraction that presupposes a more basic, time-structured engagement with the world.

Judgment belongs to this primary level. When cognition is reduced to representations, judgment is replaced by calculation.

---

## Judgment as Existential Disclosure

Judgment, in Heidegger’s account, is not a mental act added to understanding. It is a mode of disclosure: a way in which the world shows up as calling for a certain response.

This disclosure depends on the temporal structure of existence. Past experience shapes sensitivity. Future possibility gives direction. Present involvement gives urgency.

Remove this structure, and judgment collapses into rule application or optimization.

---

## Structural Implication

Judgment cannot be preserved by storing representations, decisions, or rationales alone. It depends on preserving the temporal structure of being-in-the-world: thrownness, projection, and care.

Any system that flattens this structure into snapshots preserves information while erasing judgment.
